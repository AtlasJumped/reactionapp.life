What it is

Reaction videos, made by you and your people

Watching somebody watch something amazing, or scary, or ridiculous, is fun. Reaction App makes that the whole point.

You do not have to pick one short clip and hope you react. Watch for five minutes or thirty, the way you already do. The app finds the moments your face lit up, and you keep the ones you like.

How it works

Two ways to use it

1

React to yourself

Watch longer content and get a highlight reel of your strongest reactions.

  1. Open a video app or site, and start a recording session.
  2. Watch like you always do. The camera records your face and the screen records what you watch.
  3. Press Done. You get a reel of your strongest reactions, each with what you were watching.
  4. Choose the moments to keep, then post, save, or send them.
What you watched
Your reaction

Each moment runs from about 2 seconds before the reaction to about 2 seconds after it. Moments get plain labels, like "big reaction at 1:23".

2

Send a video to a friend

Pick a video, send it, and record how your friend reacts to you and the clip.

  1. Choose a video and send it to a friend.
  2. They open it. The camera records them watching, after a clear notice and a tap to start.
  3. Their reaction comes back to you.
  4. On a desktop, you see the original clip on top and their reaction below it.
The original clip
Their reaction

Delivery between two people needs a server we have not built yet. The prototype lets you record your own reaction to a video from your device.

Highlight reel

You pick which moments stay

After a session, Reaction App scores how much your face and body moved, about four times a second, and picks the strongest peaks. You see them as a list and choose the ones to keep.

This first version is simple. It measures motion on your camera, so it can mistake a head turn or a lighting change for a reaction. A smarter reaction model will run on our own computer later, so it can tell a laugh from a sneeze.

Connect your accounts

Watch where you already watch

Instead of a clip library, you bring your own feed. In the app, each platform below has a button that opens it in a new tab. You sign in there, the usual way.

A web page cannot log in to these apps for you, and we do not pretend it can. There is no fake sign in here. We open the official site, and you share that tab when you start recording.

Privacy and consent

Your camera, your call

  • Clear notice, then a tap

    Nothing records until you read the notice and press Start. Your browser asks too.

  • You pick what is shared

    You choose the screen, tab, or window to record. Stop sharing any time from the browser bar.

  • Nothing is uploaded

    The prototype has no server. Recordings stay on your device until you save or share them.

  • Public is a choice

    You choose to share a reaction. Nobody gets posted without a yes.

A real version needs consent rules before it ships, including parent or guardian consent for minors and consent from anyone shown. We have not built those yet. We also do not copy videos from other platforms. The recording is of your own screen and your own face.

Honest status

What works today, and what does not

Works in the prototype

  • Desktop: record your screen or a tab together with your camera, with a 5 minute timer that never cuts you off
  • Phone or desktop: record your reaction to a video you pick from your device
  • A simple highlight reel: motion peaks, moments you can keep or drop
  • Save the recording to your computer, and share a link
  • Open Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, Snapchat, and X in a new tab
  • Design picker, to try five looks

Planned, not built

  • Phone screen recording, which needs the coming phone app
  • Sending a video to a friend and getting their reaction back
  • A smarter reaction model, running on our own computer
  • A real community wall, leaders wall, and messages
  • Accounts, storage, and moderation

Sessions in the prototype are limited by your browser. A long recording can use a lot of memory. Browsers also differ. Screen recording works in current desktop Chrome and Edge, and some other desktop browsers, but not on phones.
